# Escape the CPU

An original pixel-art educational runner for CSC3501. Open index.html in a modern browser; no build or server required.

Start with ENTER THE MACHINE: five automatic fade-in/fade-out story cards tell how a boy was transported into the computer, then reveal the route map (Skip story opens the map immediately). Original synthesized chiptune audio begins after this click. Toggle MUSIC at any time. Reduced-motion settings disable interface animation.

Space, Up, W, or tap jumps. Hold to jump on landing. P pauses; leaving the window pauses automatically. Existing floating platforms, gaps, accelerating movement, three revive hearts, and pickups remain. Refills appear at 500 m, 1,100 m, then every 600 m while hearts are missing.

Six timed levels (60, 90, 120, 150, 180, and 210 active seconds) follow a winding map: CPU, Control Unit, Registers, ALU, Cache, and Clock. Only the first is initially available. Reaching the exit flag starts a boss fight; defeating the bug clears the level; returning to the map unlocks the next stop. Cleared levels can be replayed. Map progress lasts for the current page session. Each level starts with a lesson and a fresh three hearts. A crash costs a heart and presents a question from that level. Wrong answers give an explanation and one recovery question; a second wrong answer ends the attempt. Retry restarts the current level, preserving map unlocks. Correct answers clear nearby hazards before resuming. Clearing the final level brings the boy home. Music and the bug chase continue across the adventure.

The character, chasing corruption bug, music, and graphics are original procedural assets. The game runs on Phaser 4.2.1. `CpuEscapeScene` in `game.js` owns the update lifecycle, and `Phaser.Game` owns the renderer and animation loop. The procedural artwork is a Phaser CanvasTexture displayed by a Phaser Image, preserving the original graphics. Custom fixed-step collision and movement run at 120 Hz from the scene update, retaining the established jumps, ramps, hazards, pauses, and level timing. Native DOM controls preserve keyboard focus, pointer capture, quiz accessibility, and responsive layout. Existing Web Audio synthesis provides music and effects.

Phaser's browser distribution and MIT license are included in `vendor/`, so opening `index.html` still works offline without a build step, CDN, or development server. The npm dependency is pinned to the same version.

Run node --test game.test.cjs for the simulated-browser regression suite. It covers physics, pause, hearts, recovery, intro, all stage gates, and victory. Run `npm install` and `npm run test:browser` for the Playwright browser checks (Google Chrome required; set `PLAYWRIGHT_CHANNEL=msedge` to use Edge). They verify the real Phaser renderer, scene and texture, jumping, pause, quiz Space handling, map controls, and mobile layout. The migration was also checked against before/after screenshots: the title screen, map, and CPU lesson matched pixel-for-pixel. Listening to audio remains a manual check.

The route map is illustrated as a motherboard with a magnified processor interior. CPU, Control Unit, register bank, ALU, cache, and clock landmarks have distinct vector icons. The Back to map button is available throughout a level, including lessons and quizzes. It freezes the attempt and asks for confirmation. Keep playing or Escape restores the exact previous screen. Confirming discards the attempt; re-entering starts that level at its beginning with fresh hearts, while completed-level unlocks remain.

The home screen now features an original pixel-art bedroom with a boy at his computer. Begin the story plays five illustrated cutscene phases: bedroom, shaking, pull/poof, trapped inside, and the escape objective. Reduced-motion settings disable shaking and fades. The animation uses Canvas primitives and shares no assets with referenced games. The soundtrack contains eight original procedural compositions: a quiet bedroom theme, Circuit Atlas for the map, and Boot Run, Signal Orders, Bit Vault, Logic Reactor, Fast Lane, and Last Cycle for the six levels. Arcade arpeggios mark trapping, teleportation, arrival, answers, and level completion. Music begins after interacting with the home screen; the music toggle mutes both music and effects. Audio quality should be checked manually with sound enabled.

The story now ends with a walk-out transition, followed by the boy stepping onto a low-resolution 400 x 220 pixel map. Use arrows or WASD to travel along the copper bus (right/up forward, left/down backward), and Enter or Space to start a level when standing at its component. Locked routes block movement. Touch users can hold the WALK buttons and use the Enter button. Optional destination buttons walk the boy to a component; they do not start a level remotely. Returning from a level places the boy at that component. Map movement stops on blur.

Dialogue now reveals one character at a time with original electronic voice blips and punctuation pauses. Story narration, lessons, and quiz explanations use this effect. Click a dialogue paragraph to reveal the rest immediately. Reduced-motion users get full text immediately; dialogue has a full-text accessibility label. Global delegated interaction audio covers buttons, summaries, destination controls, and typed text; native keyboard button activation is included. Map navigation and jumps have short synth cues. SOUND ON/OFF controls music and all effects.

Start game now fades the full-screen room to black before swapping to the cutscene layout, then fades back in. After the boy shrinks into the monitor, the camera eases toward its center and zooms through the screen. The computer interior appears inside the monitor during the zoom, aligned with the next full-screen interior shot. Reduced-motion settings bypass the title fade and camera movement.

At each exit, the runner turns to face the bug in a stationary quiz battle. Bosses have 3, 4, 5, 6, 7, and 8 HP across the six levels. Correct answers deal one damage; wrong answers consume a revive, and a wrong answer with no revives left ends the attempt. The next level unlocks only after defeating its boss. There are 48 boss-only questions, ranging from CPU concepts and control-flow scenarios to register/address calculations, binary ALU operations, cache performance, and CPI/pipeline problems. Boss battles do not count toward the running timer. The health bar and hit reaction show damage; Space cannot select boss answers. Back to map can be canceled without losing the current question or boss health.

The runner also encounters occasional tightly packed groups of two to four transistors. Later levels use longer groups, with a recovery interval after each group; each group fits within one jump.

Boss questions are docked beside the arena on desktop and beneath it on smaller screens. The arena stays visible while scrolling through mobile answers. Each level has a different bug silhouette and shell markings; defeated bugs squash into a persistent cartoon splat, with a short splash animation and sound. Reduced-motion settings show the splat immediately. Wire insulation, highlights, and shadows follow each level's palette, with exposed ends marking the jumps.

The map has Map and Review tabs. Review draws batches of 10 distinct random questions from all 138 regular and boss questions, including locked levels. Answer each question before advancing; after the tenth answer, see your batch score and start another random batch. Every ten correct practice answers earns one coin; wrong answers do not reset progress. Coins and progress are stored locally in this browser. Each question in a batch awards credit only once. Coin progress carries across batches.

During unanswered run, recovery, or boss questions, spend one coin using **Skip question**. A run skip resumes play; a boss skip deals one damage and shows the explanation. Skips do not count as correct answers or attempts in run accuracy, and do not award coins. Coins cannot be spent on answered questions or while the leave-confirmation dialog is open.
